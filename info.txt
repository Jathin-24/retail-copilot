A store manager's fundamental problem is:

Have the right product, in the right quantity, at the right store, at the right time, while not tying too much money up in inventory.

That sounds simple, but it creates several competing problems.

Problem 1 — Stockout

You have:

Demand = 20 units/day
Stock = 30 units
Supplier lead time = 7 days


The product will probably run out before the next shipment arrives.

That means:

Lost sales + unhappy customers + potentially lost future customers.

Problem 2 — Overstock

Suppose:

Demand = 2 units/week
Stock = 200 units


The retailer has money sitting on shelves.

That inventory could eventually be:

discounted
damaged
obsolete
seasonal
replaced by a newer product

So inventory is not automatically an asset just because it can be sold.

Problem 3 — Sales decline

Suppose:

Product A

January: ₹80,000
February: ₹82,000
March: ₹81,000
April: ₹54,000


A manager needs to know:

Why did sales fall?

Possibilities include:

actual demand decline
stockout
competitor activity
price increase
promotion ended
seasonality
store-specific problem
supplier issue
product substitution

A good copilot shouldn't simply say:

"Sales decreased."

It should say:

"Sales decreased 33.3% in April compared with March. 21% of the decline occurred during 6 days when inventory was zero, so the sales decline may be partly supply-driven rather than demand-driven."

That distinction is very important.

This would make your project look much more sophisticated.
